Research

Design and evaluation of end-to-end machine-learning pipelines

My research asks how data transformations, search procedures, computational effort, and selection objectives influence what an AI system learns and how it behaves.

Dissertation

Making Experimental Effort Visible in Machine-Learning Pipelines

My dissertation examines choices that are often hidden when researchers report only a final model. It develops a budgeted framework for making data-preprocessing search observable and comparable to model-tuning effort, then extends this pipeline perspective to fairness in predictive tasks and data preparation for large language models.

Together, the projects treat data preprocessing as a consequential information-quality intervention and develop more transparent ways to evaluate how data and model choices jointly shape performance and downstream outcomes.

Measuring the Hidden Impact of Pipeline Choices: Data Preprocessing, Model Tuning, and Joint Search

Aseem Baji and Jessica Clark

This work studies how experimental effort devoted to data preprocessing and model tuning affects conclusions about predictive pipelines. We introduce randomized grid search for data preprocessing and place preprocessing search and hyperparameter optimization within a shared budgeted framework. The results show that preprocessing and model tuning interact in ways that are difficult to detect when either stage is studied while the other remains fixed.

Work in progress

Preprocessing Effort and Fairness in Predictive Pipelines

Aseem Baji, Jessica Clark, and Lauren Rhue

This work studies how data preprocessing and accuracy-based pipeline search affect measured group disparities in mortgage-lending predictions. It reframes fairness as a property of the search and selection process—not only a property of the final model.

Project in development

Data Preprocessing and LLM Training

Aseem Baji

This project extends the pipeline perspective to filtering, balancing, selecting, and transforming data during pretraining and fine-tuning. It asks when conclusions about data-preparation choices remain stable across training configurations and how experimental effort should be reported.

Work in progressAkanksha Foundation collaboration

LLM-Based Personalized Math Feedback for Underserved Students

Aseem Baji, Wen Wang, and Siva Viswanathan

This work develops a system that helps educators identify student error patterns, generate worked explanations, and create targeted practice materials. Teachers remain reviewers of generated content, allowing the system to support rather than replace educators’ contextual knowledge.

Research agenda

Future directions

I plan to extend this research across fairness-aware machine learning, data preparation and evaluation for LLMs, and collaborative AI projects with social impact. Across these streams, my goal is to improve both the scientific evaluation of AI systems and the practical decisions organizations make when building and deploying them.
