Teaching

Technical fluency through application and problem solving

I help students build confidence with data and information systems by connecting technical concepts to recognizable organizational problems.

Teaching philosophy

Learning by building, testing, and explaining

I believe students learn technical concepts most effectively when they apply them, explain their reasoning, and see how individual choices affect a working system. My classes use practical examples, short periods of independent problem solving, and discussion of why alternative approaches succeed or fail.

I also encourage responsible experimentation with generative AI. Students may use these tools, but they must explain how they used them and remain accountable for evaluating the output.

Course taught

BMGT 402 · Database Systems

University of Maryland · Fall 2024 and Spring 2025

Sole instructor for two undergraduate sections serving 70 students. Designed and delivered lectures, SQL laboratories, assignments, in-class activities, office hours, a semester-long team project, and the final assessment.

Topics included SQL, SQL Server, entity-relationship modeling, relational design, normalization, query processing, data warehousing, data mining, and business information systems.

In the classroom

Active practice

Open-ended projects

Teams identify a real setting, gather requirements, design a schema, implement the database, and demonstrate its value.

Responsible AI

Students document AI use and critically evaluate generated schemas, queries, and design recommendations.
